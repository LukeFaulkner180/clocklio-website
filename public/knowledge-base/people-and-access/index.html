Manage people, onboarding and permissions

Add employees and management accounts, review starter information and control access to personal records.

Business owners · ManagersSome workflows require Full Workspace
Before you start

Your package and permissions control what appears. Team Leaders use only the sections their owner grants. Employee instructions refer to their own records; management controls need the appropriate role. Onboarding and protected personnel files require Full Workspace; wage and payroll reporting remain owner-only.

Give each person the right view

The Business Owner is the primary business manager account. It is distinct from the DayRidge platform administrator who manages the service.

Where to go Managers > Management access

RoleMain purposeKey boundary
Business Owner / General ManagerBusiness settings, team oversight, payroll and access controlOwner-level access to wage and gross-pay reporting
ManagerPlan rotas, review requests and manage worked-time recordsTeam payroll and wage reporting stay with the owner
Team LeaderPublished shifts and individually granted team toolsNo private employee files, bank details or payroll
EmployeeOwn shifts, attendance, leave and personal informationOwn records; extra tools depend on the package

Understand the three access checks

  • Package: does the business have this tool?
  • Add-on: has DayRidge enabled the purchased extra?
  • Person: does this account have the role or permission needed?

A Manager who also works shifts may have a linked employee profile and a My shifts & pay switch. Management access and an employee shift record serve different purposes; an employee profile uses an employee place in the business allowance.

Delegate deliberately

Give people the smallest set of permissions that lets them do their job. Revisit access when responsibilities change or someone leaves.

Invite Managers and Team Leaders

Create a clear management structure before handing over the working week. Access changes should match the responsibility you intend to delegate.

Where to go Managers > Add Manager / Team Leader

  1. Choose the account type. The Business Owner can invite a full Manager. A Team Leader is a more limited account with focused permissions.
  2. Enter the person’s name and email. Use an address they can access. Send the invitation and check that the management list reflects the expected account.
  3. Set Team Leader permissions. The owner chooses extra access when inviting a Team Leader or uses Edit access afterwards. New extra permissions start off unless selected.
  4. Review the actual experience. Ask the person to refresh their workspace and confirm that the expected sections appear.
  5. Remove access when appropriate. Use the relevant Remove action after checking which account and role you are removing. Do not remove the wrong person simply because names are similar.

Team Leader options

  • View team directory: active staff names and roles.
  • View open shifts and daily attendance totals, without wages.
  • Manage the team rota: draft shifts and individual publication.
  • Review annual leave: decisions and undoing upcoming approvals.
Private access stays separate

Team Leader permissions do not expose private employee files or payroll. Full Workspace personnel permissions and Stock permissions have their own controls.

Add and maintain employee records

A reliable employee record connects rota planning, clocking, leave and payroll hours. Check the basics rather than rushing through account creation.

Where to go Team > Add employee

  1. Enter the employee’s identity. Check their name, role and account email. Use the employee number assigned in the business and give them their personal clocking PIN through an appropriate private channel.
  2. Set employment inputs carefully. Review start date, regular or irregular working pattern and contracted hours or days where required. These inputs affect leave estimates and other summaries.
  3. Check owner-only pay information. The owner should verify the hourly rate used for estimates. Standard Managers and Team Leaders do not receive team wage reporting.
  4. Decide whether onboarding is required. If you already hold the information, do not ask the employee to complete the same form unnecessarily. Use the option available in the creation flow.
  5. Confirm setup. Check the new record appears, the invitation has reached the correct person and they can see their own workspace.

Plan limits and departures

Adding a person beyond the approved employee allowance can require DayRidge approval. Use the account’s upgrade/request route rather than creating duplicate records. Archiving a leaver and deleting an unused setup record are different actions; read the confirmation before proceeding.

Before the first live shift

Check the employee number and PIN on the actual workplace device. Fix account mismatches before people start using each other’s records.

Onboard a new starter

Onboarding gathers starter information for authorised review. It can be required or marked unnecessary when the business already has what it needs.

Where to go Onboarding / Employee account-setup flow

  1. 01Set requirement
  2. 02Employee submits
  3. 03Owner reviews
  4. 04Confirm outcome
  1. Explain what is needed. Tell the employee whether the onboarding form is required and where to find it. The form can contain identity, address, starter-tax and bank information.
  2. Employee completes the form. They review the requested fields and submit it. A submitted form is awaiting review; submission alone is not owner approval.
  3. Review the information. Use the Onboarding workspace to check the employee and the status. Confirm the information matches the person you intended to onboard.
  4. Approve or request changes. Follow the available review controls. If something is incomplete, explain what needs correcting rather than approving an uncertain record.
  5. Complete your business process. Make sure your approved personnel and payroll process has the information it needs. Do not treat the submitted form as a substitute for a maintained employee file.
Know the status

Not required, Not started, Awaiting review, Approved and Changes requested describe different stages. Check the status after saving instead of assuming a form has been accepted.

Employee files and personal changes

Keep personal information under the right access controls. An employee can propose changes to their own details; authorised people review the request.

Where to go Employee files / Employee > My details

  1. Open the correct record. Use Employee files and identify the employee before reviewing contact, emergency or other personnel information.
  2. Review incoming changes. Employees use My details to submit changes to supported contact and bank fields. Check the proposed values before approving a request.
  3. Separate bank-related access. Review the permission controls carefully; bank information is more sensitive than a team directory entry.
  4. Manage delegated access. The Business Owner controls which authorised Managers can access or approve protected personnel information. Give access only where their role requires it.
  5. Review profile photos. Employee profile-photo submissions can await an authorised manager’s decision before appearing to the team.

Explain this to employees

Submitting a change does not mean the business has accepted it. Employees should check the status and tell the owner promptly about time-sensitive changes. A contact-information update does not automatically decide or complete a payroll change.

A team list is not a personnel file

The team directory is for operational names and roles. It should not be used to circulate private personal, bank or onboarding information.
