Knowledge base/Stock Management
DAYRIDGE · TRAINING GUIDE

Stock counts, takings and investigations

Create consistent products, run stocktakes, reconcile till figures and explain differences.

Business owners · ManagersFull Workspace + Stock Management add-on
Before you start

Your package and permissions control what appears. Team Leaders use only the sections their owner grants. Employee instructions refer to their own records; management controls need the appropriate role.

Set up products and permissions

Start with consistent product records. Accurate units and prices matter more than a long product list with inconsistent assumptions.

Where to go Stock > Products / Managers > Stock access

  1. Confirm the add-on. Stock Management must be active for the business. The owner or authorised access manager controls which Managers can use it.
  2. Create a product. Use Add product and enter a clear name, product code or category where relevant, counting unit, purchase cost and selling price.
  3. Choose one usable unit. Count the same product in the same unit throughout opening counts, deliveries, waste and closing stock. Decide whether a unit means an item, bottle, measure or another supported unit.
  4. Review prices. Keep purchase costs, selling prices and till figures on a consistent tax and pricing basis chosen by the business.
  5. Check operational access. Give count-entry access to the appropriate Managers. Delegate access-management permission only where it is needed.

Explain the counting rules

Write down any business-specific unit convention before the first stocktake. If stock is bought in one pack size but sold in another, decide and consistently apply the conversion you will enter. The app does not automatically turn recipes, portions or yields into stock units.

Manual figures, matched periods

This version uses entered counts, movements and till amounts. Do not assume it is connected directly to your till or that it imports sales automatically.

Run a stocktake you can trust

A useful stocktake compares physical counts and relevant movements over the same period as the till sales being reviewed.

Where to go Stock > Stocktakes & reconciliation

  1. Start the record. Choose New stocktake and give the period a recognisable title. Check opening and closing count times before entering figures.
  2. Confirm the opening position. Enter the first opening count where needed or check the supported opening balance carried from the preceding record.
  3. Record movements. Account for deliveries, waste, complimentary items and relevant adjustments using consistent product units. Give a reason when a correction or non-sale movement needs one.
  4. Count closing stock. Enter what is physically present at the closing point. Recheck an unusually high or low quantity before relying on calculated usage.
  5. Match the sales period. Enter the relevant till sales for exactly the same period and products. Avoid comparing a whole-day till total with a partial-shift count.
  6. Save or complete. Save unfinished work as a draft, then reopen it when you can finish. Review the count and sales comparisons before completing the stocktake.
History matters

Keep stocktakes in a sensible chronological sequence. Later records can depend on an earlier closing count. Review dependencies before deleting or changing a linked record.

Explain stock and till differences

Use differences to start a review of the underlying figures. A variance is a signal to investigate, not an automatic explanation of what happened.

Where to go Stock > Reconciliation / Investigations / Analytics

Stock example

20 opening + 10 delivered - 18 closing - 2 waste = 10 sold.
10 × £5.00 = £50.00 expected.
£48.00 till sales - £50.00 expected = £2.00 short.

  1. Check the sign. Difference is recorded till sales minus expected sales. A negative amount is short; a positive amount is over.
  2. Check the inputs first. Review count dates, units, deliveries, waste, complimentary items, adjustments and selling prices. Recount where appropriate.
  3. Record an investigation note. Identify the product or whole stocktake, explain the discrepancy and keep an actionable follow-up. Use the supported open/resolved state.
  4. Use analytics carefully. Choose the reporting period before comparing usage, sales or product performance. Make sure the included stocktakes are comparable.
  5. Handle deleted records deliberately. Deleted stocktakes can be viewed and restored through Show deleted stocktakes. Later stocktakes that depend on the closing count must be dealt with first.
Keep the interpretation fair

A short figure may come from a count, price, timing or entry issue. Check the evidence before deciding what action the business should take.
